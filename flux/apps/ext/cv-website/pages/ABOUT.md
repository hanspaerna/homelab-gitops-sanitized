# Welcome!

My name is Hans, and I have 13+ years of experience designing, developing, and maintaining various backends for products in the music, healthcare, and telecommunications industries.

Although I am only slightly over 30, I have already had the opportunity to work in three different countries across the EU. In the end, however, I found Berlin, and Germany in general, to be the best place for me to stay permanently. I have been living and working here for more than four years now. :)

My approach to backend engineering is to start at an abstraction level much higher than the specifics of the technologies being used. I think about a system as a whole, describe its parts using proper domain language, and consider scalability and throughput before bottlenecks occur. Only then do I get closer to translating the design into an implementation.

In this respect, it would be fair to say that my work has not just been about writing code for quite some time. The thought process plays a much more important role than the implementation itself, and I find it much more interesting as well.

Among the projects I have worked on are an Estonian **public healthcare system** for hospitals and laboratories, a **microservice backend** for a Finnish telecommunications operator, a **complex ML-based system** designed to assist medical coders (Kodierung) with their work, an automated migration of a large music web store from an outdated platform to Shopify, and many more.

You can find more details about my professional experience [here](/experience).

My career started in Saint Petersburg, Russia, and my first language and framework were PHP 5 and Symfony 2. I still use PHP occasionally, but the reliability and strictness of Java, as well as the great tools provided by the Spring Framework, have pushed me toward Java-oriented positions since then. I've been working with these technologies for 8+ years.

I have also occasionally worked with Golang and Python codebases, so now I feel more language-agnostic than before.

It is also hard to ignore the rising expectation for backend engineers to be familiar with DevOps concepts and tools. I have been pretty familiar with Docker since the early days of my career, but my last two positions have raised my interest in Infrastructure as Code and Kubernetes.
I even created my own homelab with two single-node K3s clusters connected via Headscale, managed entirely through GitOps, and running on a pure Proxmox hypervisor with Terraform and Ansible.
This website also runs on it, and while it is a personal project, I am quite proud of the result. The project is open source and available on my GitHub page.

You can read more about my personal projects [here](/personal-projects).

I have occasionally participated in the development of frontend projects as well. As this website demonstrates, I am also quite familiar with the Vue 3 framework, as well as React and other ubiquitous modern frontend technologies.

I speak fluent English, Estonian, and Russian, and even a little Hebrew, thanks to my Jewish middle school. My German is somewhere between B1 and B2 at this point, but I really wish to improve it as soon as possible.

I have preferred to work from home for most of my career, as it saves time on commuting and avoids having to re-concentrate afterwards. That said, I am completely fine with occasionally visiting the office.

Just in case, I am a citizen of an EU country, so there is no need for visa sponsorship.

Currently, I am looking for new opportunities, so if you are looking for a backend engineer for your team, feel free to contact me via email or LinkedIn!
